About

My projects explore the spiritual dialogue that exists between indigenous cultures and the landscapes they inhabit. In all, I have photographed on five continents and in over 21 countries. My long term projects have taken me to the Andes (Bolivia, Peru, and Chile) and the Himalayas (Tibet, Nepal, and India) in search of the people, their unique cultural traditions, and the landscapes that inspire them.

What drives me most is the opportunity to learn from other people. Every journey begins with curiosity and a deep respect for the communities I encounter. Whether sharing a meal with a family in a remote Tibetan village or witnessing ancient ceremonies on Easter Island, I have found that the camera opens doors to meaningful connections that transcend language and cultural barriers.

Through these experiences, I have come to understand that photography is not just about capturing images—it is about listening, observing, and honoring the stories that people share. The wisdom held within indigenous traditions offers profound lessons about our relationship with the land, with each other, and with ourselves. My work seeks to preserve and celebrate these perspectives in a rapidly changing world.

I graduated from the University of Delaware in 1995 with a master's degree in electrical engineering (MSEE) with an emphasis in digital image and signal processing. Inspired by the urban landscapes of Philadelphia, my passion for photography began while attending continuing education classes at the University of the Arts in Philadelphia. Driven by a keen curiosity about what is going on in the world, I have been using the camera and the photo essay as a means of expression ever since.
